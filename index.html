Commodities

Commodities are basic raw materials and agricultural products that are produced in bulk and are largely interchangeable, whoever produced them.

Hard and soft commodities

  • Hard commodities

    Natural resources that are mined or extracted, such as metals and energy. Examples: gold, copper, crude oil and natural gas.

  • Soft commodities

    Agricultural products that are grown or raised. Examples: wheat, coffee, cotton, sugar and cattle.

Why commodities are traded

  • Producers and users manage risk: a farmer or a manufacturer may want to agree a price in advance so they can plan.
  • Price discovery: trading on organised markets brings together many views of supply and demand.
  • Diversification and investment interest: some investors look at commodities as a different kind of asset from shares or bonds.

Common ways people gain exposure

In general terms, there are several ways to gain exposure to a commodity. Each works differently and carries its own risks and costs.

  • Physical ownership: owning the material itself, for example gold bars or coins. This involves storage and insurance.
  • Futures contracts: agreements to exchange a commodity at a set price on a future date, traded on exchanges.
  • Exchange-traded funds and similar products: funds that aim to follow a commodity or a basket of commodities.
  • Shares in producers: owning part of companies that mine or grow commodities. Their value also depends on the company itself.
